Why the PREVIOUS recording was incomplete, not wrong
------------------------------------------------------
tan-cli#790 (PR #1022) shipped `sdk remove` reporting WHAT it removed but not
WHAT NOW RESOLVES in its place — deliberately deferred to this issue rather
than folded into that PR (see #1022's own body and #790's tracking issue).
After a force-removal of a load-bearing install (the machine-global default or
a workspace's project pin), the removal envelope gave no signal what `sdk
current` would report next; a caller had to make a second `tan sdk current`
call to find out, with no way to tell from the removal response alone whether
the workspace is now unpinned, falls through to a different tier, or has
NOTHING left to resolve — the case a consumer most needs told about.

`resolvesToAfter` answers that by re-running `resolve_sdk_tiered` (the SAME
narrow ladder `sdk current` itself calls) for the calling workspace, mirroring
`sdk current`'s own `data` shape (`sdkPath`, `readiness`, `sourceTier`) rather
than inventing a fourth one. It is present on EVERY `sdk remove` response —
idempotent-absent, every refusal, every failure, and a real removal alike —
not only a load-bearing one: an always-present field is easier for a consumer
to code against than a conditional one, and this hermetic offline-absent case
is exactly the shape a fully unpinned, no-SDK-anywhere workspace reports both
before and after (`sourceTier: "none"`, matching `sdk-current-no-sdk`'s own
pinned shape). The behavioural cases where it reports something OTHER than
`"none"` — a still-live project pin before removal, and falling through to a
surviving lower tier after a forced one — are covered by
`test_sdk_command.py::test_remove_refuses_the_active_install_without_force_and_force_clears_it`
and
`test_sdk_command.py::test_remove_reports_the_lower_tier_it_falls_through_to_after_a_forced_removal`,
neither of which this hermetic golden can reach (both need a real filesystem
target to remove).
